How it works

Each organization is its own workspace. Nothing you list or request is visible outside the organization.

  1. 1

    List what your group doesn't need

    Just upload a photo, the quantity, condition, pickup location, and when it's available for pickup. An admin will do a quick review before each listing goes live.

  2. 2

    Request what you need

    Say what your group needs, how many, and by when. Choose pickup or delivery and then mark if a similar item or a partial amount would be good.

  3. 3

    Review a matched plan

    LoopLocal proposes a plan across one or more groups and explains why each one is good. Submit it for approval by admin and then arrange the pickup or drop off.

LoopLocal's personal matching engine

The Resource Rescue Engine follows written rules. It is not artificial intelligence: this means if you put the same request with the same available supplies, you'll get the same plan every time.

  1. Filtering. The system only looks at approved items that are still in stock from other teams in your organization, fit what you're looking for, and match your deadline and pickup times.
  2. Ranking. Exact matches come first, then substitutes, then better condition, and finally plans with fewer separate pickups.
  3. Combining. If one group doesn't have enough to cover your whole request, the system pulls supplies from a few different teams to make it work.
  4. Explaining. Every single line in your plan tells you why that specific item was picked.

On top of this, two requests can never accidentally have the same supplies. The system checks quantities inside a database transaction before anything is confirmed.

Organization admins

Decide what needs sign-off, keep teams and members organized, and watch how much money and gear your group saves over time.

Why reuse matters

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency calls source reduction, which includes reusing items, the most environmentally preferred way to manage materials. It lists these benefits: less harmful emissions, saved natural resources, conserved energy, less pollution, less toxic waste, and money saved for consumers and businesses. 1

The scale is large. Americans generated 292.4 million tons of municipal solid waste in 2018, and only 32.1 percent of it was recycled or composted. 2 Keeping a usable item in circulation inside your own organization is the first step before anything reaches the bin.
